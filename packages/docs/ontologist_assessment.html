Ontologist Capability Assessment

Rubric self-rating, then adaptive probes to confirm it, then a staged plan from wherever you land. No pass mark.

1 · Self-assessment
2 · Adaptive confirmation
3 · Where you are & next steps
Progress saves automatically in this view.

Pick the statement that sounds most like you on each dimension — the one you'd say out loud, not the one you'd like to be true. The next stage tests it either way, so over-rating just makes the assessment longer. Levels: 0 Absent · 1 Understands concepts · 2 Works with guidance · 3 Works independently. There is a fifth level — sets direction — that you can't claim here; it is only reachable by answering the hardest probe on a dimension. There is no pass mark: this maps where you are and what the next step up looks like.

Each item is pitched at a specific level. Answer correctly and the next steps up; miss it and the next steps down. No level is decided by a single answer — a miss at or below your own rating gets a second item at that level, a split gets a third, and whatever turns out to be your ceiling has to hold twice. Expect three to five items per dimension, around 40 in total.

Confirmed profile

Calibration column compares the self-rating with what the probes confirmed. Persistent over-rating is itself a finding.

Development order

Ordered by how far the dimension sits from independent practice, weighted. Not a ranking of failures — a suggested sequence.

Learning plan

Staged from where you are: each step lists what to do and the statement you should be able to make once you've done it.

Rubric covers the core ontology-engineering skill set: RDF/RDFS and OWL 2 foundations, SHACL validation, ontology architecture and layering, competency-question elicitation, SPARQL, source-to-concept mapping, governance and provenance, and novel-domain investigation. Item bank holds 108 items — 9 dimensions × 4 levels × 3 — each with five options, and is editable: see BANK in the page source. Options are shuffled on every presentation, and each sitting draws different items, so the same person can be reassessed later without measuring memory.
